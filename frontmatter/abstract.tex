\begin{center}\huge \textbf{Abstract}\end{center}

The exponential growth in data demand for high-performance computing, cloud datacenters, 
and graphics processing units (GPUs) necessitates robust high-speed serial interconnects. 
This project presents the design and implementation of a comprehensive PCI Express (PCIe) Gen 6.0 Serializer/Deserializer (SerDes) Physical Layer (PHY) Transceiver, 
operating at 64 GT/s. As PCIe standards transition from Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ) to Pulse Amplitude Modulation 4-level (PAM4) signaling, 
managing severe frequency-dependent channel attenuation, severe crosstalk, and stringent linearity requirements presents profound signal integrity 
challenges across the full physical link.

To achieve reliable high-speed data transmission, this thesis details both the transmitter (TX) and receiver (RX) architectural paths. 
On the transmit side, emphasis is placed on high-bandwidth serialization and precise multi-tap feed-forward equalization (FFE) to pre-shape the pulse 
and mitigate precursor distortion before injecting signals into the high-loss backplane channel.

On the receive path, a high-performance Analog Front-End (AFE) is implemented to reconstruct the degraded PAM4 eye diagram. This work covers 
the optimization of wideband termination networks utilizing bandwidth extension techniques, such as T-coils and bridged-shunt peaking, 
alongside highly linear Variable Gain Amplifiers (VGAs) configured to maximize dynamic range. 

Central to the receiver's equalization strategy, a multi-stage Continuous-Time Linear Equalizer (CTLE) is custom-designed using source degeneration and 
active topologies to provide wideband programmable high-frequency peaking, counteracting linear inter-symbol interference (ISI) with minimal latency and 
power consumption. To handle post-cursor ISI without introducing noise amplification, a high-speed Decision Feedback Equalizer (DFE) is integrated following 
the AFE block. The DFE architecture leverages predictive or look-ahead techniques to overcome critical loop-latency constraints inherent to 64 GT/s processing. 
The entire transceiver PHY is evaluated across process, voltage, and temperature (PVT) corners to ensure robust eye opening, low bit-error rates (BER), and full 
compliance with the rigorous PCIe Gen6 specifications.
